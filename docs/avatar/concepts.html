How the accessories fit (58 of them, in six spots)

Every avatar names the same spots: hat (top of the head), eyes, face (top lip and nose), cheek, neck and back (behind him, legs and all), plus hand, where the flag and the props go. Each accessory is drawn once and placed on each direction by its spot's position and size, so a new hat works on every body without being redrawn. One item per spot. They ride on the body, so they bob and lean with him, but sit outside the eyes so a blink doesn't squash the glasses.

His own bits. "His own" in each spot is what the avatar comes with there: the cat's collar, the owl's beak, the TV's buttons. Wearing something in that spot takes his own piece off, so the two never sit on top of each other. A bow tie replaces the cat's collar, and a moustache shrinks the owl's beak to a little tip above it. Each card lists what its avatar has.

The hat decides what happens to the antenna: a party hat hides it (the pompom stands in), a beanie or a top hat lets it poke out of the top, and headphones go over it. The animals have no antenna, so a hat sits between the cat's ears, the owl's tufts or the dino's spikes.

Choosing an avatar and dressing him are separate: the picker swaps the drawing, and the wardrobe stays as it was, so a top hat bought for the owl is still on when you switch to the cat.

Choose your Tatty

How choosing an avatar could look in Tatty's settings, with the same list in his right-click menu (Avatar ▸). Polished Tatty is everyone's to start with. The rest are tried on here and unlocked in the shop window below. Whichever one is chosen does everything the others do and wears the same accessories: the wardrobe in the bar above dresses him.
